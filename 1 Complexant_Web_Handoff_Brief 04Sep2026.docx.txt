One primary reader, one secondary gatekeeper. The site should be written for the first and give them ammunition for the second.
Primary: the quality leader
* Heads of Quality, Regulatory, IT, and Validation, plus CTOs and COOs, Director level through Chief Quality Officer.
* Pharmaceuticals, biologics, cell and gene therapy, medical devices, and IVD (in vitro diagnostics).
* Mid-size and emerging companies, Phase 2 and beyond. Any stage of maturity, from paper-based systems through predictive analytics. We are not opposed to partnering with large companies; they are simply not the primary target.
* GMP-fluent, curious about AI and innovation, and wary of it. They have sat through vendor demos that could not answer an inspector's first question. They personally answer for "who approved this, and on what evidence?"
Secondary: the gatekeepers
* Financial, cybersecurity, and regulatory decision-makers who sign off on spend.
* The copy has to hand the quality leader the words to win the CFO conversation: predictable cost, reduced inspection exposure, no science project.
What clients are buying
A boutique practice with roughly 60 years of combined experience in the exact roles they hold. Personal involvement from both principals, not a bench of juniors. The ability to say yes to fast-moving innovation and still be able to prove it.

Hard rules on color
* Hierarchy: blue largest, teal next, copper smallest, fog gray recessive.
* Copper carries meaning. It marks the critical point, the apex, the thing that matters most. If copper appears more than once or twice in a single view, pull it back.
* Never pure black in graphics. Charcoal #2B2F33 is the floor and must read distinctly from black.
* Warm Ivory and Pale Teal sit close together. Do not rely on those two alone to separate adjacent elements; add a rule, a shadow, or a label.
* If you propose a dark mode, tell us in the quote. We have not specified one. The reversed logo exists, so the mark is not a blocker.
6. Logo, signal, and wordmark
Three elements are in scope for the website. A fourth brand graphic, the heat map node, is deliberately excluded from this hand-off; do not use it and do not price for it.
The Signal (the icon)
* A field of crisp vertical rounded-end lines that scatter on the left and converge left-to-right into a single copper point.
* Each line is built from stacked palette color segments, so the lines have internal gradation rather than one flat fill.
* Copper appears sparsely across the field and concentrates at the output point on the right.
* Direction is fixed. Never flip, rotate, or mirror the convergence.
* It sits on a transparent background, so it embeds inline as an icon or bullet without a panel.
The Wordmark
* Set as COMPLEXANT PARTNERS LLC, with no comma or trailing period.
* Agatho and Poppins are what the delivered wordmark is built in. Both font files and their licences ship with our logo package to download and use. 
* COMPLEXANT is set in Agatho, a high-contrast display serif. 
* PARTNERS and LLC are set in Poppins, uppercase and widely tracked.
* Poppins is the body copy face across brand materials and is what the site body type should use.
The two X treatments
Signal-X. The X in COMPLEXANT is not a typed letter. The signal lines begin spread out over the E, then grow denser and converge to a pinnacle that forms the X itself. The convergence is the X. This is the full lockup and the primary site logo.
Copper-X. The typed version, for anywhere the signal graphic is impractical: running prose, email, headings, plain interface text. Write the name normally and color only the X in Copper #C7742D, in the same face and weight as the rest of the name. Where color cannot be controlled, the name is simply Complexant with no special treatment.
The scattered X is a separate asset. It exists now in full-color as an editable vector (AI and SVG) plus PNG exports. A copper version of both the scattered-X wordmark and the scattered-X full logo is in production with our logo designer and will follow. Treat the scattered X as the standalone X mark, useful small, in isolation, or as a repeating brand element. It is not a substitute for the full lockup.
Usage rules
* Clear space around any lockup equal to the height of the Signal icon, or the cap height of COMPLEXANT. Nothing intrudes.
* Minimum size: full logo no smaller than about 180 px wide on screen. The icon stays legible to about 32 px; below that use the icon alone.
* Light artwork on Warm Ivory or white. Reversed artwork on Complexant Blue, Muted Teal, or Charcoal. Always strong contrast, never over a busy photograph or a busy fold of a texture.
* Do not recolor outside the palette, stretch, skew, rotate, add effects, or rebuild the wordmark in a substitute font.

8. Voice and copy rules
These apply to every word on the site, including microcopy, buttons, alt text, and metadata. They are not stylistic preferences; several of them are positioning decisions.
Avoid term “control” unless part of tool name, QC or intentionally worded/provided
We avoid using "control" or control framing to describe our own role. We guide, advise, verify, and equip. The client decides, flies, and is accountable. The approved line is: "You stay pilot in command. You decide and you fly; we guide, advise, verify, and equip."
We are not an AI company
We focus on compliance, regulatory intelligence and quality around innovation, including automation and AI. We will leverage AI, guide in the AI journey, but also experts in non-AI Q&R.
The aviation thread
There is an aviation thread in our internal language (pilot, instruments, flight plan). Partnering together on their Journey is core. No puns or pictures of planes.
Vocabulary
Preferred: guide, advise, verify, evidence, prove, judgment, competency, clarity, fit-for-purpose, phase-appropriate, adopt then adapt.
Avoid/Intentional use: controlled, revolutionary, cutting-edge, game-changing, best-in-class, seamless, landscape, transformational. If a claim needs an adjective, it needs evidence instead.
One correction to carry forward
The line "Complexity, controlled." appears on some earlier internal materials. It is retired and breaches the rule above. If you see it in anything we send you, ignore it and tell us where you found it.
